Intake is where the build quality starts. Good intake gives you enough clarity to write a decision log and spec without guessing. Weak intake gives you polished confusion that turns into rework.

## Intake question categories

Run intake in categories so your notes can become a usable brief.

### 1. Business basics

- What is the business name and who owns it?
- Where is the business located and what area does it serve?
- What is the primary action you want a visitor to take?

### 2. Customer and problem

- Who is the core customer?
- What are they trying to solve when they land on the site?
- What objections or confusion usually come up before they buy?

### 3. Services and scope

- What do you offer now?
- What should be emphasized first?
- What should be intentionally out of scope for this phase?

### 4. Voice and trust signals

- What should the site sound like in your own words?
- What language should never appear on your site?
- What proof points should be visible early, testimonials, years in business, credentials, outcomes?

### 5. Content and assets

- What photos, logos, copy drafts, or docs already exist?
- What is missing and who will provide it?
- Are there legal or compliance facts that need verification before publish?

### 6. Logistics and constraints

- What is your desired launch window?
- Who approves content and technical decisions?
- Are there platform constraints, integrations, or handoff requirements?

---

## Brief generation flow

Once intake is complete, move notes into a brief in this order:

1. Normalize raw notes by category.
2. Pull direct quotes that define voice and customer reality.
3. Mark missing facts and unresolved decisions explicitly.
4. Draft a one-page build brief with scope, goals, constraints, and open items.
5. If notes are vague, stop and schedule a follow-up intake pass before writing the spec.

<Callout variant="key-rule">
  Never smooth over vague intake with invented detail. If the notes do not support a confident brief, stop and run the follow-up.
</Callout>
